The money rule, and the one deviation the brief invited

Every figure goes through one formatter — Indian grouping, two decimals, the sign outside the symbol, never an abbreviation. The brief’s example delta ↑ ₹12.4 L this week was therefore rendered as ↑ ₹12,40,000.00 this week; where a full figure would crowd a compact stat, the delta is a percentage or a count. Imprest shows figures because site cash is not a statutory computation; TDS, payments, billing and retention stay in their setup state with no figure.

One grid, one card, one form per fact — 19 September 2026, the grid

Today and Overview sit on twelve columns with a 24px gutter, in the rows a director acts in: the hero at twelve; four tiles at 3·3·3·3 — margin at risk, payables this week, overdue receivables by how long overdue, unsigned variations; the money owed each way at 6·6 with the bar in ageing buckets; money in and out at 8 beside cash by account at 4; ordered against contract beside milestones; site today beside your day; spend by trade package beside the pipeline. Measured before: three tiles at 259, then 461·329, 395·395, 461·329, 395·395, and one 395 with a hole beside it — five widths and a lone card. The gate reads every child of a grid against its tracks.

One card header, 48px, one line: the duotone disc, the title, its help icon, and one action or a period picker at the right. The two-line strip of the same morning went. A title is at most 24 characters and never wraps; on a six-column card under 1000px the action and the picker fold to their icons so the line still holds; a tile’s action stands at its foot, because three columns cannot hold a title and an action on one line at any width a laptop has. The disc — 28px, the icon at 20 — sits first in the header of a tile and a money card and nowhere else in a card; measured before, it sat in four places on one page (right of a figure, far right of a total row, inside a sub-card, left of a hub title). One figure scale: hero 48, total 28, tile 24 (the hero was 56 at the widest).

One form per fact. The six rings under the meter list drew the same six numbers a second time — gone. Approvals ageing was a column chart of 3 · 1 · 2 — gone; the hero says 2 older than a week. Past its contract ceiling repeated KRA-01’s 127% a third time — replaced by unsigned variations, the action rather than the problem. On Overview the client’s pending variation is the tile, not a list as well, and the agreement’s stages are the milestones panel.

No internal text in a product frame. Cash by account printed HUMAN(ARCH-CASH) … CA-gated and unbuilt inside the demo. Where the seed cannot produce a panel’s data the panel is drawn absent and the gap recorded, never a number invented: no report is dated today, no stage carries a date, no variation carries a sent date, no lead an expected close date. The alignment gate

align.mjs renders this file in headless Chromium and asserts with getBoundingClientRect: buttons in a group share a top and a height; tiles in a row share a height; a table column's cells share both edges; numeric columns are right-aligned on one x and money columns share a decimal point; a label and its control start on one left edge; pills sit on the text baseline; an icon beside text is centred on that text's x-height; nothing is wider than its container outside a declared scroll wrapper; the same component renders with identical padding and radius everywhere; every product screen carries exactly one hero; every empty state is the published anatomy — an image, a heading, one description of one or two sentences, at most two buttons with the primary last, at most one link; every drawing obeys the drawing system and is decorative; and the document's own section list keeps one line per label, one left edge and one right edge for the numbers. It runs at 400, 768, 1280 and 1440px in both themes, twice — with the web fonts loaded and offline on the fallback stack — and reports zero faults.

The CA step, reframed — and the architecture note behind it

The shipped product frames tax as a per-tenant verification: “confirm your rules with your CA”. That restates the objection this design exists to remove. Statutory rates are the same for every Indian business, so they are verified once, centrally, and shipped as a versioned set; what a tenant confirms is its own handful of facts — GSTIN, state, whether it deducts TDS — which is a two-minute setup step, not an audit.

For the build team, an architecture note, not a design one: this implies the verified rate set (the M2.5 spec, ADR-0014) is seeded per deployment and versioned, not entered per tenant. The tenant’s three answers are the only per-tenant tax data. The values in section 11 are illustrative; the build seeds them from the verified spec, never from this file.

The retheme — 16 September 2026

The owner withdrew the rule that this product takes enterprise conventions but keeps its own look. It is now drawn in a published enterprise design system’s colour, elevation, spacing, radius, type scale and dark mode. The structural patterns taken earlier — the object switcher, the record header, the list with a record beside it — stay, and every screen’s information design and value line survive unchanged. Where each value came from, when it was read, and the 40 values our gates moved are in 0 · Foundations. These are the decisions it forced.

One system owns colour

Colour, and the token structure it comes in, is taken whole from one system; the patterns borrowed from the other are shapes, and bring no colour with them. No hue from a second palette appears anywhere, and the build fails on any colour written outside the token file. The other system was weighed for colour and not taken: it publishes no categorical chart sequence with dark-mode and colour-deficiency guidance, and adopting its colour would have meant adopting its component vocabulary with it. The system taken publishes every semantic token in light and dark, a chart palette with dark steps, and elevation as surfaces paired with shadows — the parts a repaint actually needs.

Blue is the brand and every affordance; it is never a status

Blue carries the primary action, a link, the focus ring, a selected row, the current page — and one status, in progress, as the published lozenge draws it. Information as a sentence is a section message: a tinted surface with an icon and a heading, never a lozenge, never inline text, never a button. Measured, the brand and the four status inks paint at least 43.5° apart in both themes.

Red, amber and green now come from the system — and red still reads as loss

Red on a money screen means money going the wrong way, and nothing softer. That is the convention an Indian finance reader already brings: bank statements, accounting software and the market apps used every day put a loss or a debit in red. Red’s ceremonial meanings — a wedding, a festival — live outside a ledger, and this product never uses red as decoration, as the brand, or for emphasis, so it has no other reading to borrow. Red is also never alone: a figure going the wrong way keeps its minus sign or its words — over, short, refused — and the India gate lists every red element with its text so none can carry the meaning by colour. This was reasoned, not tested with users, and is marked so. Caution moved inside the system’s own ramps to stay distinct from loss and from done: in light, every step of its orange sat within 25° of the red, so caution takes the yellow ramp; in dark, the published caution sat under 40° from done and moved one step down its own ramp.

The flag, re-measured. Saffron beside green on white is the flag. In light, caution sits ΔE 28.1 from saffron and done ΔE 9.7 from India green, on tinted fills with no white between them. In dark, caution ink is an orange ΔE 4.1 from saffron — close — but it is text on a near-black fill, with no white and no green band beside it, so the three-colour reading the flag needs is never present. The illustration accent, which used to be the nearest thing to saffron in the system, is now magenta: ΔE 25.7 in light and 23.1 in dark.

The look the buyers already know — 18 September

The theme is the one our buyers run their books in. They already keep their online books and Tally open all day, so the app is drawn to feel like the books — the reference is named in the brief, not here, as the system the colour comes from is not: a dark top bar holding the product, the search, the project switcher and one blue + New; a light sidebar with an icon on every item, chevrons on its sections and the current page a solid blue pill; white cards under a light-grey header strip with a title, a help icon and one action; stat tiles with an icon on a tinted disc; money cards with a proportion bar and the split beneath; and lists whose title is the view’s name with a chevron, whose filters follow View by, whose heads are small capitals and whose names are links. Type, surfaces, focus

  • Inter, in every family, under the SIL Open Font License. The system’s own typeface is licensed to its owner and is not used; nor its logo, its product names or its icons — every icon here is this product’s own, and no screen names the system.
  • Cards are flat, with a border. Raised — a surface and a shadow — is spent once per screen, on the hero or the record open beside a list.
  • Focus on a button or a link is a 2px ring set 2px outside the control, 3.50 : 1 on the card in light and 8.11 : 1 in dark. The glow drawn around the ring before is withdrawn.
  • Navigation’s selected state is a solid pill — the bold selected fill under the inverse ink, 5.20:1 in light and 6.00:1 in dark — as the reference the buyers know draws it.

That is the whole information-architecture change, and it is what takes sixteen sidebar items down to seven plus Settings.

What happens when there are four thousand of them

Every table in this file is hand-set at about a dozen rows, which is how a design file flatters itself. A buyer with three years of history has 4,000 purchase orders. The pattern is specified once here and applied to every list screen in the document.

  • The row count is always stated, in the pager, in the form Showing 1–12 of 83 files. When a filter is on it also says what it was filtered from — 1–3 of 3 items filtered from 53 — because “3 items” and “3 of 53” lead to different next moves. When nothing matches it reads No items, never 0–0 of 0.
  • Pages, not infinite scroll. Fifty rows a page. A paginated list has a position you can return to, a URL you can send someone, and an end; an infinite list has none of those, and on a purchase-order history somebody is always looking for the thing they saw last Tuesday. Infinite scroll is also the pattern that quietly breaks Ctrl+F, which is how half of site staff actually search.
  • Sorting and filtering are server-side, and the URL carries them. Sorting the visible page client-side is the defect that looks like a feature: it silently sorts 50 of 4,000 rows and presents the answer as if it were the whole set. One exception, stated: a list the server has already declared complete — under about 200 rows, which covers vendors, projects and rate contracts for most tenants — may sort in the browser, and then the pager says all 61 rather than a range.
  • While a page loads the table keeps its height and its header, and the rows become the skeleton shown in States. It does not collapse to a spinner: a table that changes height on every page turn moves the pager out from under the pointer.

Responsive, as a rule rather than three screenshots

Six media queries render three widths, which proves nothing about the fourth. The rule for a wide table is a column priority order, declared per table, and the browser drops from the bottom of it as the width runs out.

  1. Identity never drops. The column that says which record this is — the order number, the item, the vendor, the project code — is the first column and it is never dropped, never truncated to an ellipsis, and never moved.
  2. The decision column never drops. Each table names one: Check on rates, Level on stock, Issues on daily reports, Ordered against contract on projects. It is why the screen is open.
  3. Money and status drop after those, and when they drop they move into the row’s detail line rather than disappearing — a second line under the identity, which is where the phone layout puts them already.
  4. Reference columns drop first, and if a column can drop at 400px without anybody noticing, that is evidence it did not deserve the width at 1440 either. Four columns failed exactly that test and were cut from the document rather than made responsive.

So the order for Buying › Rates is: Item · Check · Agreed rate · Last paid · BOQ rate — and at 400px the last two become the detail line. The order is a property of the table, written next to it, not a stack of breakpoints.

Keyboard

The web app is for staff who live in it all day, so the keyboard is a specification and not an afterthought.

  • / focuses the search box from anywhere that is not already a text field. ? opens this list. Esc closes the top-most thing — the drawer, then the popover, then the filter — and never navigates.
  • Focus order follows the page: skip link, search, the page’s primary action, the toolbar, then the table. The sidebar comes after the content, not before it, because a person who lives on one screen tabs into the work far more often than into the navigation.
  • A table is one tab stop, then ↑↓ move the row and Enter opens it. Tabbing through 50 rows × 6 cells is the reason people stop using the keyboard. Space selects a row, Shift+↑↓ extends the selection, and Ctrl+A selects the page — the pager then offers select all 4,000 explicitly, because those are different intentions.
  • The bulk bar is reached by tabbing forward from the table — it appears above it visually but after it in the DOM, so it is not something you tab through to get to the rows. Esc clears the selection and returns focus to the row it started on.
  • Single-key shortcuts can be turned off, per person, in Your preferences › Keyboard. With them off, / and ? type themselves and the list of shortcuts is in the account menu.
  • The sidebar is a disclosure, not a menu: Tab through what is visible, Enter or Space opens a section, ↑ ↓ move between visible entries, ← → do nothing. The whole specification is in 3.
  • A decision is never a single keystroke. Approve and Decline have no shortcut. Everything reversible is fast; everything that signs something is deliberate.
  • The focus ring is the same everywhere — 2px solid, offset 2px, measured at 3.50:1 in light and 8.11:1 in dark against the card — and it is never removed on mouse users, because :focus-visible already tells the two apart.

Could not verify

  • Every measurement is Chromium on Windows: with Inter, and on Segoe UI without it. Other platforms fall to their own sans; the sizes hold, the metrics may differ by a pixel.
  • The published token values are marked by their owner as subject to change, and three things used here were behind feature flags on the day they were read. The read date is on every value in 0 · Foundations; a later read may differ.
  • That red reads as loss to this product’s Indian users was reasoned from the conventions they already use, not tested with them.
  • The gate measures at the four widths and spot-checks 600, 1024 and 1680; widths between them are not proven.
  • Equality is ±0.5px; a fault under half a pixel is invisible to the gate and to the eye.
  • The hero check reads the largest font size and the largest card; it cannot judge whether the hero is the right thing — that was decided screen by screen and is listed in the report.
  • The Google Fonts stylesheet is the one network request in the file. Offline the page renders on the fallback stack; a viewer who has never been online sees Segoe UI, by design.

Constant contrast, not constant chroma — a rule that was wrong, and its evidence

An earlier version of this document held every semantic colour to one chroma, 0.082, on the reasoning that a set at one chroma cannot have a member that shouts. The evidence that it was wrong is the colour it produced: --warn at hue 52 rendered #935f3f, a brown, sitting 39° from --bad at identical lightness and chroma — so caution read as a faded version of loss. Freed to the chroma sRGB actually offers at the same lightness, hue 52 renders #9d4b00, a burnt orange, at the same contrast. The pinned chroma was the defect, not the lightness.

The mechanism is that the sRGB gamut is not uniform. At L 0.535 an amber reaches chroma 0.139 and a violet 0.278, while the brand teal reaches 0.088. Holding one chroma across the set means holding every hue to the narrowest one — which is the brand's — and a teal's ceiling is no basis for what an amber should look like.

The rule Was Is
The invariant chroma, held at one number contrast, held at one ratio — because contrast is what accessibility actually requires
Chroma pinned across hues as much as the gamut gives at the lightness that contrast implies
Lightness spread capped under 0.06 whatever the contrast target needs, per hue
The bound on chroma the brand's own ceiling matched perceived weight, judged on rendered swatches side by side
Hue separation 40° minimum, measured on the declared hue 40° minimum, measured on the hue the pixel actually paints

The measured result: every semantic colour sits at 5.18–5.21:1 in light and 5.93–5.97:1 in dark against the worst surface it appears on, while their chromas differ — 0.130 for amber and green, which are at their sRGB ceiling, against 0.149 and 0.150 for red and violet, which are held back to match. That bound of 0.15 was not calculated; six candidate policies were rendered as real pills on the real panel and looked at, and 0.15 is where the four read as one family instead of four strangers.

The last rule of the old set has been withdrawn too: the brand is no longer required to be the most chromatic thing on screen. That rule was a consequence of constant chroma — the brand set the ceiling precisely because one number had to serve every hue. With per-hue chroma the comparison stops being meaningful (0.15 at hue 52 and 0.15 at hue 305 are not equally colourful), and enforcing it would mean amber cannot be amber, which is the defect being corrected. What replaces it is narrower and says what the rule was actually protecting: the brand out-saturates every piece of chrome — the hairlines, the inks, the skeletons, the neutral fills, the track — and it remains the only hue in navigation, action, focus and selection. Since 18 September the accent tokens carry meaning beside the chrome — a tinted disc behind a stat’s icon, green and red on money coming in and going out, the yellow ramp on what is overdue, the purple tint on a row waiting on you — and none of it is blue, so the sentence before this one still holds. A caution that cannot out-shout the chrome cannot do its job, so it is not asked to lose to it.

Caution is a fill carrying an ink, and it is never a stroke — amended 19 September: the fill is for a lozenge and a section message only; amber may be text

Amended 19 September 2026 — no highlighter. The owner’s rule: a figure, a label or a sentence never sits on a coloured fill. The caution fill is for a lozenge and for the published section message, and nothing else; caution as text is allowed — an overdue figure carries an amber small-capitals label above an ink figure, a warning is amber text with its icon — and the contrast gate holds it to 4.5:1 (--warn measures 5.93:1 on the card in light and 8.36:1 in dark). The alignment gate’s rule was rewritten to that, not lowered: it now fails a highlighter, and still fails amber on a stroke or a border. The paragraphs below are the reasoning that stood until then, and the stroke-and-border half of it still does.

Amended again, 19 September 2026 — the charts. A chart mark is the third place the caution fill may go, on one condition. The overrun band past a meter’s track, the overdue part of an owed bar, the at-risk segment of a stacked bar: each is a mark whose value is printed beside it — “₹13,17,120.00 over”, the overdue figure beneath the bar, the legend’s count — so it is labelled-redundant, and it takes the fixed status amber, color.background.warning.bold, the same in both themes. No text sits on it, so it is not a highlighter; and the non-text gate grants the class by finding the value in the mark’s card, not by reading a comment — a mark it finds no value for is held to 3:1. The amber that was #946F00 in light and #DDB30E in dark — a yellow darkened to 3:1 on white is olive — is gone from every mark.

This is the resolution the gamut forces. Every lightness at which amber looks like amber is a lightness at which it cannot hold text contrast: measured on this document's own panel, hue 52 at its gamut maximum gives 5.23:1 at L 0.54 (a burnt orange), 3.24:1 at L 0.66 (a real orange, non-text only) and 2.04:1 at L 0.78 (true amber, and invisible). So caution comes from the area, not from a thin mark:

  • --warn-soft is a large light amber fill — this is the thing that reads as amber.
  • --warn is the dark amber that sits on top of it and carries the contrast, measured at 5.23:1 in light and 6.65:1 in dark against its own fill.
  • Amber appears as no stroke, no border, and no text floating on a panel. Five places broke that rule and were converted: a stat's delta went to ink, its note became a chip, a stat border was deleted, a due-today marker became a chip, a low-stock figure went to ink, and a caution notice's icon went to ink because the notice is already an amber area.

The alignment gate proves it rather than trusting the stylesheet: it walks every element in both themes at four widths and fails the build if caution is painted on a border or a stroke, or if caution ink is found anywhere that is not sitting on a caution fill.

The light and dark caution are different colours, and that is correct. Light is #9d4b00, a burnt orange, because on a near-white panel that is the lightest amber that still holds contrast. Dark is #ff9c5c, a genuinely bright amber, because against a dark panel the constraint inverts and amber finally has room at high lightness. Every other token in this palette is the same parameters at two luminance levels; this one pair is not, and nobody should later "fix" it into agreement.

Amber at 52° beside marigold at 72°

Twenty degrees apart, and the answer is containment, not distance. They never co-occur: amber is a status area inside a table or a stat, marigold appears only inside an illustration, and an illustration only appears on an empty state — a screen with no rows and therefore no pills. The gate that proves marigold never escapes the sprite is the same gate that proves they cannot meet. Moving amber further from marigold would have cost it the separation it needs from red, which is the one that matters, since bad and caution do sit in the same column. That separation was also wrong until now: bad at hue 12 and caution at 52 read as exactly 40° in the generator and painted 39.0°, because 8-bit rounding lands hue 12 at 12.7. Bad moved to 10 and the check now measures the hue the pixel has, not the hue it was asked for. The pair paints 42.4° apart.

Charts have their own architecture

The deeper error was that charts were borrowing the status palette. They should not, and the reason is one sentence: a status pill has text beside it, and a chart line does not. "Above agreed rate" says what it is, so its colour is redundant reinforcement and a low-chroma, weight-matched set is exactly right. A line has no such help — colour is its only channel — so it needs more discrimination than a status colour, not the same. Applying the pill rules to charts is what put two chart states 39° apart at identical weight and made them one state.

So the chart set is its own, and no chart references a status token — a rule the gate enforces by walking every element inside every chart in both themes and failing on any status colour it finds:

Token What it is
--series-1..3 Categorical identity, at hues 196 · 254 · 94. One hue set for both themes, because colour follows the entity and a series may not change identity when the theme does. The warm band 30–90° is excluded: amber is the caution status and marigold the illustration accent, and a chart series is neither.
--chart-point-bad A saturated red point for a genuine failure. One point, never a line — so red never means "series 4" either.
--chart-threshold The neutral dashed rule saying where the limit is. A limit is not a status.
--chart-band A low-opacity caution fill over the out-of-tolerance region. An area, which is the one shape amber can hold.
--track Deliberately not a chart token. It is the unfilled part of any bar — meter, progress, setup — and renaming it into the chart set would have made a setup progress bar reference a chart colour.

No line is coloured by condition any more. A line that turns red says "everything is bad", which is almost never what the data means, and it throws away the ability to say where the problem is. The line carries series identity and the exception is drawn instead — a shaded band over the out-of-tolerance region, a dashed rule at the limit, a diamond at the point it was crossed, and the row's own words. Six sparklines were dyed amber by a warn tone. Three had a real limit and now draw it: cash against payables crosses zero, a contract crosses its 85% watch line, and a stock item crosses its reorder level. Three had no limit anyone had agreed, and the honest outcome is that they lost the colour and gained nothing — a mute threshold line on a series with no threshold would be decoration pretending to be information.

The same move fixed Ordered against contract. A project past its contract used to turn the whole bar red. Now the bar keeps its identity, the run past the contract is a band, the crossing carries a diamond, and the row’s title reads what it is past the contract by — which is the thing a director actually wanted and the recoloured bar never said.

Simulated for deuteranopia and protanopia, because red against amber is the common confusion

Every chart in the document was re-rendered through both dichromat simulations and measured pair by pair. Zero charts carry meaning that survives only in full colour vision. The chart set itself holds up without any help: its tightest pair measures OKLab ΔE 13.8 to a dichromat in light and 10.5 in dark, against a floor of 8, and that is the number that matters because a chart line has no text beside it. Three sparklines do fall under 8, and all three are the same pair: the teal line against the neutral threshold rule, at ΔE 3.3 under protanopia. That is legitimate and deliberate — one is solid and the other is dashed, so the channel separating them was never hue. Every exception in the document carries at least two non-colour channels: a shape (the diamond), a pattern (the dash), an area (the band), and words.
